A guide to winter layering

By Ana Gil · Outdoor skills, beginner

Three layers keep you warm outdoors: a base that moves sweat away, a mid layer that traps heat, and a shell that blocks wind and rain.

Base layerMid layerShell

Base layer

The base layer sits against your skin and moves sweat away from it. Merino wool (150–200 g/m²) stays warm when damp and doesn’t smell after a long day; synthetic polyester dries faster and costs less. Avoid cotton: it holds water and pulls heat from your body, which is how a cold day turns dangerous. A snug fit matters more than thickness, because fabric that doesn’t touch the skin can’t move moisture.

Mid layer

The mid layer traps warm air. A 100-weight fleece works for most days between −5 and 5 °C; a light synthetic insulated jacket (60–100 g/m² fill) adds warmth for stops. Down is warmer for its weight but loses most of its loft when wet, so keep it for dry cold and breaks, and carry it in a dry bag. Open the zip before you overheat instead of after, so you don’t soak the layer from inside.

Shell

The shell blocks wind and rain. A waterproof-breathable jacket with pit zips handles changing weather; a windproof softshell is enough on dry, windy days and breathes better while you climb. Check that the hood fits over a helmet or a warm hat and that the cuffs close over gloves.

“Be bold, start cold.”

Start the hike slightly cold: after ten minutes of walking you’ll be warm, and you won’t have sweated into your layers.
